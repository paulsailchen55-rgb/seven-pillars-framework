# Seven Schools and Curriculum

## School 1: Creation & Stewardship

Purpose: teach people to build, cultivate, innovate, and maintain.

Core questions:
- What are we creating?
- What resources does it require?
- Can it last?
- What will it leave behind?

## School 2: Restoration & Compassion

Purpose: teach recovery without abandoning truth or responsibility.

Core questions:
- What has been lost?
- Who has been affected?
- What can be repaired?
- What support is needed for recovery?

## School 3: Human Development & Mentorship

Purpose: develop capability and transfer knowledge.

Core questions:
- What can this person learn?
- What can this person teach?
- What support removes unnecessary barriers?
- What ability is being wasted or misdirected?

## School 4: Provision & Belonging

Purpose: ensure basic needs and meaningful participation.

Core questions:
- What is needed?
- Who can receive it?
- Who is excluded?
- Is provision becoming control or hoarding?

## School 5: Reconciliation & Accountability

Purpose: repair relationships and systems through truth and responsibility.

Core questions:
- What happened?
- What evidence exists?
- Who is responsible?
- What must be repaired?
- What does forgiveness require?

## School 6: Wisdom, Direction & Restraint

Purpose: teach responsible use of authority and capability.

Core questions:
- Who has authority?
- What limits apply?
- What safeguards exist?
- When should we stop?
- Can the decision be reversed?

## School 7: Protection, Preservation & Teaching

Purpose: preserve life, knowledge, resources, and future choices.

Core questions:
- What must be protected?
- What must be preserved?
- What knowledge could be lost?
- What future options must remain open?

## Curriculum Method

A simple lesson can follow:

**See -> Name -> Understand -> Practice -> Review -> Repair -> Teach**

The framework favors practical exercises over abstract slogans. Students should be able to identify a real problem, locate it within the seven responsibilities, identify risks and responsibilities, propose a constructive action, review the result, and teach what they learned.

## The Child Test

An explanation passes the child test when a person with no specialized background can answer:

1. What is happening?
2. Why does it matter?
3. Who is responsible?
4. What choices exist?
5. What happens next?
6. Can we stop or go back?
7. How do we protect the people affected?

This is not a test of intelligence. It is a test of whether the system communicates clearly enough to be usable.
