# Ethics, Scope, and Limitations

## Symbolic Framework, Not Established Science

The Seven Pillars Framework is a synthesis and teaching model. It combines biblical source material, name-meaning traditions, professional analogies, and original secular interpretation.

It should not be presented as a scientific proof, a historical reconstruction, or an established psychological classification.

## Name Meanings

Ancient names can have disputed, uncertain, or multiple proposed etymologies. The framework therefore uses symbolic working meanings and identifies uncertainty where appropriate.

## Genesis 10

The Genesis 10 list is not treated as a modern census. Ancient categories of peoples, regions, cities, and tribes do not map cleanly onto modern nation-states or ethnic identities.

The secular capability categories are intentionally separated from modern populations.

## Harmful Capability

The framework proposes that a person may possess a capability that has been directed toward harmful ends. This does not mean harmful behavior should be excused.

The required sequence is:

**Identify capability -> protect people -> establish accountability -> redirect capability -> evaluate results**

Protection and accountability come before any attempt at rehabilitation.

## Forgiveness

Forgiveness does not mean:
- denying what happened;
- removing necessary boundaries;
- abandoning victims;
- eliminating consequences;
- hiding evidence;
- avoiding repair.

The framework treats forgiveness as compatible with truth, accountability, protection, and restoration.

## Protection

Protection should preserve life, dignity, and future choice. It should not automatically become permanent control.

## Secular Use

Public-facing versions can use terms such as:
- sustainability;
- recovery;
- compassion;
- human development;
- provision;
- accountability;
- restraint;
- safety;
- continuity;
- dignity;
- preservation.

Religious source material can remain documented as the historical and conceptual origin without requiring a public audience to share the same faith.

## Evaluation

The framework should be tested against real cases. It should be revised when:
- categories overlap unnecessarily;
- a pillar produces contradictory guidance;
- a proposed action causes avoidable harm;
- the model becomes too complicated to use;
- symbolic interpretation is mistaken for historical fact;
- a proposed solution removes more future choice than it preserves.

A useful framework should become clearer through testing, not merely larger through accumulation.
