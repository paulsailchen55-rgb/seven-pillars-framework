# Professional Applications

The framework is designed to be translated across professions without pretending that the professions are identical.

## Human Development

The human level asks people to:
- create and care;
- recover from loss;
- learn and teach;
- provide and receive;
- tell the truth and repair;
- exercise judgment and restraint;
- protect and preserve.

## Software Engineering

### 1. Create & Sustain
Architecture, maintainability, scalability, resource efficiency, lifecycle planning, and sustainable systems.

Questions:
- Can another engineer understand this?
- What does the system consume?
- What happens when dependencies disappear?
- Is the design repairable?

### 2. Restore & Care
Disaster recovery, backups, migrations, resilient services, accessibility, continuity, and recovery of broken user experiences.

### 3. Develop & Teach
Documentation, onboarding, mentorship, human-centered interfaces, accessible information, training, and knowledge transfer.

### 4. Provide & Belong
Infrastructure, storage, bandwidth, processing, identity, communication, access, and reliable resources.

### 5. Account & Repair
Testing, observability, logs, incident review, root-cause analysis, responsible remediation, and transparent learning.

### 6. Direct & Restrain
Permissions, governance, safe defaults, controlled deployment, feature limits, human review, and stopping unsafe automation.

### 7. Protect & Preserve
Security, backups, disaster recovery, interoperability, documentation, succession, and protection against single points of failure.

Compact engineering sequence:
**Architect -> Recover -> Develop -> Provide -> Account -> Restrain -> Protect**

## Nursing

### 1. Create & Sustain
Whole-person care, safe environments, dignity, prevention, and supporting health.

### 2. Restore & Care
Recovery, rehabilitation, transitions, continuity, and compassion.

### 3. Develop & Teach
Patient education, explanation, self-care, warning signs, empowerment, and understandable information.

### 4. Provide & Belong
Nutrition, hydration, rest, mobility, hygiene, comfort, communication, medication continuity, and safe discharge.

### 5. Account & Repair
Assessment, documentation, advocacy, communication, recognizing missed signs, and preventing repeat errors.

### 6. Direct & Restrain
Clinical judgment, prioritization, escalation, scope, safe practice, and avoiding unsafe shortcuts.

### 7. Protect & Preserve
Patient safety, medication safety, dignity, information, continuity, independence, and prevention of avoidable harm.

Compact nursing sequence:
**Care -> Recover -> Teach -> Provide -> Assess -> Judge -> Protect**

## Soldier

Prepare -> Recover -> Train -> Sustain -> Account -> Restrain -> Protect

Examples include personnel readiness, recovery and reintegration, training and mentorship, logistics, after-action review, lawful authority and control of force, and protection of people and future security.

## Athlete

Train -> Recover -> Develop -> Support -> Evaluate -> Discipline -> Preserve

Examples include training, injury recovery, coaching, nutrition and team support, performance review, rules and self-control, health, integrity, and longevity.

## Farmer

Cultivate -> Restore -> Teach -> Provide -> Observe -> Manage -> Preserve

Examples include soil, seed, animals, water, biodiversity, food production, knowledge transfer, observation of results, resource management, and preservation of productive land.

## Why the Same Framework Works Across Professions

The framework does not say that a nurse should act like a software engineer or that a farmer should act like a soldier. It identifies a common responsibility structure beneath different forms of work.

A profession can emphasize several pillars at once. The seven categories are therefore a map, not a rigid job description.
